# Sample Riff to Beat

## When to use

Use this to build a new beat or arrangement around an owned riff, loop, melodic fragment, percussion pattern, or other cleared audio source.

## Recommended model

- Default: `v6`
- Experimental flip: `v6-wild`

## Inputs

- Cleared source audio
- Timestamp of the riff
- Whether to preserve pitch, rhythm, timbre, or groove
- Target genre and tempo
- Rules for drums, bass, and harmony

## Recipe

1. Use only audio you own, created, or have permission to sample and transform.
2. Isolate the best timestamp and describe it in words.
3. State whether the source should remain recognizable or act as a hidden texture.
4. Build the beat around one source function: hook, rhythm, bass, atmosphere, or call-and-response.
5. Ask for original drums, bass, and surrounding arrangement rather than a soundalike of an existing track.

## Copy-ready template

```text
Build an original beat around my cleared source audio. I own or have rights to use this sample.

Source:
<file name>, timestamp <mm:ss-mm:ss>.
Element to use: <riff / bass note / percussion / texture>.
Recognition level: <clearly featured / partially transformed / hidden texture>.

New production:
<genre>, <tempo>, <drums>, <bass>, <harmony>, <structure>.

Preserve the source's <pitch/rhythm/timbre/groove>.
Create original surrounding parts and do not imitate any named song or artist.
```

## What to preserve

Preserve one source feature with intent. If pitch, rhythm, and timbre are all forced to remain, the new beat may have little room to develop.

## Failure signals

- The output sounds like a copy of a known track.
- The sample is buried under unrelated drums.
- Timing changes make the groove unstable.
- The model treats background noise as part of the riff.

## Next iteration

Shorten the sample, specify its bar placement, and choose one preservation target instead of several.

## Evidence

Evidence tier: `official` for sampling and rebuild capabilities described in the v6 announcement; `official-informed` for clearance and prompt structure.
