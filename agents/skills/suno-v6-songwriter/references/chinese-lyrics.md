# Chinese Lyrics

## Core rules

- State Mandarin, Cantonese, or bilingual before writing.
- Keep each line around one breath and one image.
- Prefer natural spoken word order over dense written Chinese.
- Put the emotional keyword near a phrase ending or repeated note.
- Use simple pronouns and consistent tense.
- Spell numbers as words when singing them is important; avoid long number strings.
- Treat punctuation as a breath cue. Use commas for short lifts and periods for phrase endings.
- Limit English inserts to short rhythm sounds unless the user is intentionally writing bilingual pop.

## Mandarin ballad pattern

```text
[Verse]
<seven to ten characters: concrete image>
<seven to ten characters: emotional reaction>
[Chorus]
<repeatable phrase>
<repeatable phrase with a small variation>
```

## Cantonese electropop pattern

- Write by sound, not only by meaning.
- Keep ending particles and rhyme words in stable melodic positions.
- Prefer concise hook lines that leave space for synth gaps.
- Avoid forcing Mandarin syntax into Cantonese lyrics.

## Bilingual hook pattern

```text
<short Chinese emotional phrase>
<one or two English syllables as answer>
<short Chinese emotional phrase>
<one or two English syllables as answer>
```

Keep one language dominant in each section. Do not switch languages inside a fast phrase unless the user explicitly wants that effect.

## Diction repair

1. Shorten the line.
2. Move difficult consonant clusters to a slower note.
3. Make English a background answer.
4. Add one delivery cue, such as "whispered", "open vowel", or "spoken softly".
5. Test with `v6-mini`, then polish with `v6`.
